What Scholarly Engagement does for you

We are the department that connects your teaching and research to the library's collections, instruction, and services. One point of contact, four kinds of help.

Collections

We select, purchase, and analyze the education materials your courses and research depend on — print, ebook, streaming, and database.

Instruction

Course-embedded sessions, research consultations, and asynchronous modules built to your assignment, not a generic tour.

Research support

Literature searching, systematic and scoping review support, citation management, data services, and publishing questions.

Outreach

Events, exhibits, and author programming that put your students' and your own work in front of the wider Tulane community.

Place-based use

The New Orleans signal is strong

Education titles with Louisiana or New Orleans subject headings account for 425 loans across just 67 titles — roughly 6.3 loans per title, nearly three times the education average.

Public schools

Charter & privatization

Segregation & desegregation

Segregation in New Orleans education, and African American education history in the South.

What this means for selection: a small, sharply local collection is doing outsized work. Tell us what you're assigning on New Orleans education and we will deepen it.

Interpretation

What we take from the data

  • Declining print circulation is not declining need. It typically reflects a shift toward ebooks, articles, and course-provided readings — not that the discipline stopped reading.
  • Concentrated use beats broad use. Education's high loans-per-title on local and equity topics tells us to buy deeper in fewer areas rather than thinner across many.
  • Course-driven spikes are visible. Subjects like critical pedagogy and multicultural education move when they are assigned. Syllabus visibility helps us buy ahead of demand.
  • We would rather ask than guess. This analysis sets the agenda for a conversation with you, not a weeding decision made without you.

Send us your Spring syllabi or a reading list and we will check holdings, flag what's missing, and purchase where the policy allows.

Part Two

Services for your courses

Instruction, asynchronous modules and tutorials, purchase requests, and the textbook policy that governs what we can buy for your students.

Instruction

Sessions built to your assignment

Course-embedded sessions

In person or over Zoom, scheduled to land the week before the assignment is due rather than in week two. We'll ask for the prompt in advance.

Research consultations

One-on-one appointments for your students — and for you. Common asks: literature searching, database strategy, and getting unstuck on scope.

Graduate & thesis support

Systematic and scoping review methods, citation management, and search documentation for students writing at length.

Data services

Finding education datasets, working with public Louisiana data, and data management plans for funded work.

Request instruction: complete the library instruction request form

Lead time: two weeks is comfortable, one week is workable, and we will always try to say yes to the week-of request.

Required Textbook & Course Reading Collections Policy

We are now purchasing textbooks

To promote equal access to course readings, librarians can purchase select required course materials — books, chapters, articles, and textbooks assigned by the instructor.

We can acquire a single copy when it is

  • Available as an ebook, paperback, or hardcover
  • Available for institutional use
  • For ebooks: unlimited-user access
  • For ebooks: DRM-free

Decisions also weigh

  • Librarian discretion
  • Fund availability
  • Class size
  • Course frequency

Print textbooks and other assigned print materials can also go on Course Reserves — faculty fill out the reserves form. Streaming media for course use goes through Media Reserves.

Setting expectations

What we're unable to purchase

Excluded materials

  • Titles with supplemental instructional materials separate from the textbook, including online interactive applications
  • Titles unavailable for institutional purchase from our vendors
  • Loose-leaf or unbound titles
  • Ebooks over $500.00 or print books over $200.00

Publishers who don't sell digital textbooks to libraries

  • Cengage Learning
  • McGraw-Hill Education
  • Oxford University Press
  • Pearson Higher Education
  • Sage Textbooks
  • Elsevier imprints, including Elsevier Health Science, Mosby, and Saunders

Minor revisions made by the SE Collections Working Group, March 19, 2026.

New this year

Children's Literature and Culture

Newly acquired from Adam Matthew, and directly relevant to Education, English, and History courses.

Rare books, games, ephemera, and artwork from the late eighteenth to early twentieth centuries documenting the cultural history of childhood in the United States. Materials range from mass-produced chapbooks to richly illustrated volumes, dolls, paintings, coloring books, and scrapbooks — sourced from the Children's Literature and Graphic Arts collections at the American Antiquarian Society and the Winterthur Museum, Garden and Library.

Teaching idea: pairs well with courses on literacy, childhood, curriculum history, and children's literature — and with the author programming on the next slide.

Let's build something together

Outreach collaboration

Authors Among Us: Exploring Children's Literature by Tulane Faculty and Students brought School of Professional Advancement education faculty member Richard Mihans together with his students Libby Shadoan and Cassidy Williams to discuss their journey publishing children's literature.

Scholarly Engagement hosted it in Howard-Tilton Room 430 with an online option, and it is exactly the kind of program we would like to run again with Education.
